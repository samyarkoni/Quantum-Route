COACHING EFFECT VS PLAYER EFFECT IN THE NFL
Quantum Route research note -- September 27, 2026
Data: 2018-2025 regular seasons (4,254 team-games), nflverse schedules for coaches / starting QBs.
Code: Code/Team_Model/coach_vs_player.py   Raw output: Data/Team_Model/coach_vs_player_results.txt

==================================================================================================
1. BOTTOM LINE
==================================================================================================

"Coaching vs players" has different answers depending on what you measure:

  What is measured                      Head coach    Starting QB    Rest of roster/staff
  ------------------------------------  ------------  -------------  --------------------
  Offensive efficiency (EPA/play)          ~10%           ~59%              ~31%
  Pass rate over expected (scheme)         ~14%           ~59%              ~27%
  4th-down aggressiveness (decisions)      ~59%            ~9%              ~33%

  (Share of the between-team variance on offense that each source explains; posterior means.
   90% intervals are wide -- see section 3.)

  * EFFICIENCY IS A PLAYER STORY, AND MOSTLY A QUARTERBACK STORY. One SD of QB quality is worth
    ~0.076 EPA/play (about 4.7 points per game over ~62 snaps); one SD of head coach ~0.030 EPA/play
    (about 1.9 points), and the coach interval nearly reaches zero.
  * DECISION-MAKING IS A COACHING STORY. 4th-down aggressiveness follows the head coach across QBs
    and teams (Dan Campbell, Nick Sirianni most aggressive; Jon Gruden, Bill Belichick, Mike Tomlin
    least). The QB barely matters for it.
  * "SCHEME" (PASS RATE OVER EXPECTED) FOLLOWS THE QB MORE THAN THE HEAD COACH. This is the
    surprising result. Part of it is a data artifact (scrambles are recorded as runs), but most of
    it survives the correction; the likelier story is that coaches adapt the call mix to the QB
    they have. Pass rate is a coach decision that is largely *determined by* the QB.

This matches the published literature: coaches matter, but in the NFL they explain a minority of
team outcomes, while the QB is the single largest identifiable input to offensive performance.

==================================================================================================
2. WHAT PUBLISHED RESEARCH SAYS
==================================================================================================

Berry & Fowler, "How Much Do Coaches Matter?" (MIT Sloan Sports Analytics Conference, 2019).
  Estimate leader effects across MLB, NBA, NHL, NFL, college football and college basketball with
  randomization inference that accounts for player quality and schedule. Across sports, coaches
  account for roughly 20-30% of the variation in team outcomes. In the NFL they find coach effects
  on points allowed and point margin, and significant effects on fumbles and penalties; coaches
  matter more in college football (where they also move points scored and wins) than in the pros.
  Read for this project: NFL coach effects are real but modest, and show up in discipline-type
  outcomes (ball security, penalties) as much as in scoring.

Brian Burke, "On the Effect of Coaching" (Advanced Football Analytics, 2013).
  Argument from variance decomposition: after randomness and player talent, "there is very little
  variance left" for coaching. NFL coaches are near-interchangeable at the top because staffs are
  large and best practices spread quickly; coaching shows more on the downside (avoiding disasters)
  than the upside. No point estimate given.

Eric Eager, "Building a Way to Measure NFL Coach Value" (SumerSports, Nov 2023).
  Mixed-effects model on pre-game point spreads with random effects for QB and head coach (the same
  structure as our analysis, but on the market's view of strength instead of on-field EPA). The
  best coaches are worth only ~1.3-1.5 points of spread (Belichick +1.48, John Harbaugh +1.37,
  Andy Reid +1.29), with wide credible intervals.

Lopez, Matthews & Baumer, "How Often Does the Best Team Win?" (Annals of Applied Statistics, 2018).
  Bayesian state-space models on betting-market data. Relevant context: game-to-game randomness
  is large relative to talent differences in the NFL, which caps how much of any season's results
  can be attributed to anyone, coach or player. Our "game" variance component is the same idea.

Yurko, Ventura & Horowitz, "nflWAR" (Journal of Quantitative Analysis in Sports, 2019).
  Multilevel models on public play-by-play to credit individual offensive players (EPA / win
  probability) -- the methodological basis for crediting players rather than teams. QBs dominate
  offensive WAR.

Romer, "Do Firms Maximize? Evidence from Professional Football" (Journal of Political Economy,
  2006). Using dynamic programming on 1998-2000 play-by-play, teams kicked on 4th down far more
  often than win-maximizing play would dictate -- the canonical evidence that 4th-down behavior is
  a coaching *decision* with systematic (and changeable) biases.

Fourth-down trend reporting (FiveThirtyEight; Sportico 2024; NFL Next Gen Stats Decision Guide).
  League go-for-it rates sat near 10% of 4th downs from 2006-2017, then climbed after 2018; teams
  went for it 886 times in 2025 (1.63 per team-game) vs 476 in 2015. Detroit under Dan Campbell
  posted the three highest single-season go rates (~30%, 2021-2023). When the NGS model favored
  going for it by 2+ points of win probability, teams went 31% of the time in 2017 and 53% in 2020.
  Consistent with our finding that go-rate over expected is a head-coach trait.

Pass rate over expected (PROE). Standard public definition (nflfastR xpass): actual pass rate
  minus a model's expected pass rate given down, distance, field position, clock and score.
  Public commentary usually treats PROE and pace as play-caller traits; one analysis reports pace
  moves with the play-caller more than the QB. Our data complicates that for PROE (section 3).

==================================================================================================
3. WHAT OUR DATA SAYS
==================================================================================================

Method.
  Each team-game outcome y is split into crossed random effects, fit with the project's hand-rolled
  Metropolis-within-Gibbs sampler (Code/Team_Model/mcmc.py):

      y[game] = mu + coach + QB + roster(team-season) + opponent(opponent-season) + game noise
      each effect ~ Normal(0, tau_group);  game noise variance = per-play variance / n_plays + omega^2

  - coach  = head coach that game;  QB = starting QB that game (nflverse schedules)
  - roster = everything constant within a team-season: the other 21 starters, coordinators, staff
  - opponent absorbs schedule strength; omega absorbs game-level noise (weather, game plan, luck)
  - Outcomes: offensive EPA/play (from our EP model, garbage time excluded); pass rate over expected
    on 1st-3rd down (league logistic model with down, distance, field position, score, clock);
    4th-down go rate over expected (same features). Shares are tau^2 of each offensive source over
    the sum of coach + QB + roster.

  Identification comes from movement: 13 head coaches led 2+ teams, 68 of 82 coaches started 2+ QBs
  (injuries and benchings), and 79 of 150 QBs started under 2+ head coaches. The backup-QB games
  (same coach, same roster, different QB) are the cleanest contrast in the data.

  Convergence: 4 chains x 10,000 draws (thinned by 4); every parameter R-hat <= 1.009, ESS >= 347.
  The first attempt did NOT converge (R-hat up to 2.09): small variance components produce the
  classic hierarchical "funnel". A joint rescaling move for (tau, effects) fixed it (see
  mcmc.scale_update); results were stable between the unconverged and converged runs.

Model-free check: year-over-year correlation of team-season averages, by what changed.

                              pairs   EPA/play   Pass rate o.e.   4th-down go o.e.
    same coach, same QB        109      0.47         0.68              0.56
    same coach, new QB          59      0.22         0.14              0.37
    new coach, same QB          27      0.19         0.55              0.12
    new coach, new QB           28     -0.16        -0.14              0.23

  Read across rows: keeping the QB preserves pass-rate identity (0.55) far better than keeping the
  coach (0.14); keeping the coach preserves 4th-down identity (0.37) far better than keeping the QB
  (0.12). EPA/play loses persistence whichever one changes. Small samples (27-59 pairs), so treat
  as corroboration, not proof.

Scramble check (is the QB effect on pass rate just labeling?).
  In this data scrambles are recorded as run plays. From 2023 on, coverage is charted on the runs
  that were scrambles (7.5% of "runs", averaging 7.6 yards vs 4.5), so those seasons can be redone
  as dropback rate. On 2023-2025:
      as labeled:                       coach 31%, QB 48%, roster 21%
      scrambles counted as dropbacks:   coach 28%, QB 43%, roster 30%
  Relabeling trims the QB share a little; the QB remains the largest source. (The coach share is
  larger in these three seasons than over 2018-2025, but the intervals overlap heavily.)

==================================================================================================
4. INTERPRETATION AND CAVEATS
==================================================================================================

  * "Head coach" is not "play-caller". Many head coaches (especially defensive ones -- Staley,
    Tomlin, Belichick) do not call offensive plays, and offensive coordinators change more often.
    OC effects are inside the roster/team-season term here. Play-caller data would sharpen the
    pass-rate result; the head coach is the right unit for 4th-down calls, which HCs make.
  * The analysis covers the OFFENSE only. Defensive-minded head coaches may add value on the other
    side of the ball that this decomposition does not see (Berry & Fowler find NFL coach effects on
    points allowed).
  * Coaches choose QBs and adapt to them. When a coach passes more with his starter than with the
    backup, the model credits the QB. "QB effect on pass rate" should be read as "pass rate moves
    with the QB", not "the QB decides".
  * Selection: coaches who keep jobs, and QBs who keep starting, are the good ones; movers may
    differ from stayers. Random-effects shrinkage handles small samples, not selection.
  * EPA/play is not wins. Coaching value in clock management, 2-point and timeout decisions, and
    player development is mostly outside these three outcomes.
  * 2018-2025 only. Earlier eras (pre-analytics 4th-down behavior) would show different shares.

==================================================================================================
5. WHAT THIS MEANS FOR QUANTUM ROUTE
==================================================================================================

  1. Team strength should be built from PLAYERS, led by the QB. When a starting QB is injured or
     benched, the team's offensive EPA prior should move by the QB-effect difference (~0.076
     EPA/play per SD, ~4.7 points/game), and the market often under- or over-adjusts on backup
     starts. Next step: add a QB term to team_strength.TeamStrengthModel so a QB change moves the
     offense prior immediately instead of waiting for recency weighting to catch up.
  2. 4th-down tendencies should follow the HEAD COACH. On a coaching change, carry the coach's
     go-offset to the new team rather than the team's history (TeamTendencyModel currently keys
     on team; keying the go-offset on coach is a small change).
  3. Pass-rate tendencies should follow the QB-coach pair, weighted toward the QB.
  4. Coaching adds little to point-spread prediction on its own (~1-2 points for the best coaches,
     per both our EPA estimates and Eager's spread model). Its main value to the simulator is
     through decisions (4th downs), which change game variance and totals more than means.
  5. Offseason carryover (data.recency_weights, carryover=0.5) should depend on what changed:
     same coach + same QB warrants much more carryover (year-over-year r = 0.47 for EPA) than a
     team that replaced both (r ~ 0).
