How a pick is made

The same inputs always give the same pick. Simulations are seeded by game ID, and the rule below has no randomness or judgment.

  1. Model line. The team model simulates each game about 400 times, fitting only on plays before that week, and gives a projected home margin m and total t. Calibrated multiplies m by the slope that maps model margins onto real ones (the model is too timid). That slope comes from the other backtested seasons, so a season never sees its own results.
  2. Win probability. Each outcome is treated as normal around the model line, with the spread of real misses from the other seasons. Home covers with Φ((m − line)/σ), the over hits with Φ((t − total)/σt) and home wins outright with Φ(m/σ). Pushes are ignored.
  3. Expected value. EV = p × (decimal odds − 1) − (1 − p) per unit, using the listed price. Spreads and totals without a listed price, or with a line you have edited, use the default juice.
  4. One choice. Of the six bets on a game (both sides of each enabled market), take the highest EV. If it clears the minimum edge, bet it, otherwise pass. Ties go to spread, then total, then moneyline.
  5. QB change only (optional). Bet only games where at least one team starts a different QB than in its previous game, per nflverse's listed starters. In 2019–2025 backtests the model went about 56–57% against the spread on these games, a slice chosen after looking at the data. This switch exists to paper-trade it from here on, not because it is proven. Starters for future weeks usually appear a few days before kickoff.

A positive EV only means the model disagrees with the market. Use the track record above to judge whether those disagreements have made money.
